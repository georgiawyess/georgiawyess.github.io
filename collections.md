<h3>Collections</h3>

<p>The following is a collection of quotes, graphs, and papers that have been compiled by me over the years. This compilation was started in high school with the goal of putting together pieces of wisdom that meant something to me. Gradually, this list has grown and eventually I framed the many pieces of paper and have taken these ideas with me to my college dorm, first apartment, and beyond. I've referenced many of the ideas presented here in conversations amongst friends, and I like to believe that they've shaped a portion of my thinking. These ideas are presented below.</p>

<p>[1] “Every time you victimized someone,” I said, “you were victimizing yourself. Every act of kindness you’ve done, you’ve done to yourself. Every happy and sad moment ever experienced by any human was, or will be, experienced by you.” - <a href = "https://www.galactanet.com/oneoff/theegg_mod.html" target ="_blank">Andy Weir, <i>The Egg</i></a></p> 
<p>---</p>

<p>[2] Schwartz, Martin A. (2008). The importance of stupidity in scientific research. <i>Journal of Cell Science, 121</i>, 1771. <a href="doi:10.1242/jcs.033340" target="_blank">doi:10.1242/jcs.033340</a></p>
<p>The ideas in this essay carried me through some of my lowest points in my research. I find this to be good life advice, and something to live by while conducting research across all fields.</p>

<p>[3] From Graham Weaver's Last Lecture, Stanford University:
  <ol>
    <li>Do hard things.</li>
    <li>Do <i>your</i> thing</li>
    <ul>
      <li>Life is suffering so chose something worth suffering for.</li>
    </ul>
    <li>Do it for decades --- <i>passion + stamina</i></li>
    <ul>
      <li>In the equation (R+1)^n, the <b>n</b> is the most important part, be selective about what you're going to focus your time on.</li>
    </ul>
    <li>Write your story first, then make it happen</li>
     <ul>
      <li>Working backwards is critical because it gives you a goal to focus on. Adopt an "it will happen because I will make it happen" mindset and keep these three rules in mind:</li>
      </ul>
        <ul>Write your story as if it will begin five years from now. Treat it almost as a timeline.</ul>
        <ul>Start your narrative as if you know you cannot fail.</ul>
        <ul>The "how" is the killer of dreams. Achieving goals requires a bit of delusion anyways.</ul>
  </ol>
  Don't be afraid to dream.
  </p>
